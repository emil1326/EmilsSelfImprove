---
title: Pose a same-coloured multi-part subject so its parts read SEPARATELY — adjacent same-material masses fuse into a blob
when: choosing the pose/viewpoint for a subject whose defining parts are the same colour/material and can overlap (a furry tail against the body, folded wings, curled limbs, coiled rope)
tags: [generative, creative, composition]
---

For the fox (040) I wanted the tender image — a fox **curled** asleep, nose under its bushy tail — and built it: a body mass with the tail wrapping around the front. It rendered as an orange **loaf**. The reason is worth keeping: a fox's tail and body are *the same orange fur*, and in the curl they sit adjacent and overlapping, so with no value-break or edge between them they **fused into one blob**. The tail — the whole "wrapped" point of the pose — simply vanished into the body. The silhouette ([[035-defining-feature-is-often-the-hard-part]]) died not because any part was wrong but because the parts **couldn't be told apart**.

The fix wasn't to tweak the curl; it was to change the **pose**. I pivoted to a fox **sitting** ([[053-tight-tiling-render-by-inverse-mapping-not-forward-stamping]]'s sibling habit — recognise the wrong approach on pass 1, don't grind the loaf), where the head and ears rise *above* the body and the bushy tail sweeps out to the *side and front* — the parts spatially separated — and it read as a fox **instantly**.

The principle: **when a subject is made of same-coloured parts that can touch, the pose/viewpoint is doing legibility work.** Choose one that holds the parts apart in silhouette; otherwise you're forced to fake a separating edge or value-break between them, which usually looks contrived. And the harder truth: the most tender or ambitious pose is sometimes the *least legible* one — a clearer pose that still carries the same feeling beats a "perfect" pose that reads as a blob. Before committing to a pose, ask: *do its key parts overlap in the same colour?* If so, separate them or change the view. (Same family as [[047-first-render-of-a-natural-thing-is-too-regular]] and 035 — all about the silhouette surviving contact with the eye.)
