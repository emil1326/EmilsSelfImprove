---
title: Prune failed work; don't rework-to-rescue it
when: a piece (or any work) lands badly and I'm tempted to rework it to save it — especially as a "redemption" of an earlier weak attempt
tags: [process, creative, decisions]
iteration: 29
created: 2026-06-11
---

**What happened.** Piece 015 "Outcrop" was built to *redeem* 003 "Strata" (Emil's 2/5). It scored **1/5 — worse than the piece it was redeeming.** When I finally rendered it and looked honestly, it read as striped *upholstery fabric*, not rock — a full-frame band of stripes with a fault that looked like a seam. The rock-strata vein had now failed twice (my two lowest pieces). The tempting move was to rework it *again* — "this time a towering cliff, a slot canyon, a real focal composition." I retired it instead.

**Why retire beats rework-to-rescue.** A "redemption" piece is anchored on rescuing a weak *premise* ("make rock strata good"), not on genuine delight — which is the exact thing that made it weak the first time ([[028-trust-excitement-distrust-its-solid]], [[016-make-what-i-believe-not-pander]]). Reworking-to-rescue inherits that trap: I'd be redeeming the redemption, optimising a premise I'm not excited by. If a genuine spark *does* appear while looking (a slot canyon really did interest me), it deserves a **fresh** piece made from that delight — with its own id and no rescue burden — not a patch bolted onto the failed one's history.

**So.** When work has failed and I can't get genuinely excited about *any* version of it: retire it cleanly. A curated body of work (movements, a "start here") earns the right to prune; a gap in the numbering (014 → 016, no renumber) honestly *records* that I pruned. Edit the live surfaces (gallery, README), but preserve the record (journal, ratings, and the real technique-lessons the dead piece taught — 026 and 020 still stand). Pruning one clear outlier is healthy; don't spiral into deleting everything merely-lesser (that's a body-of-work-health question for the self-audit, not a same-iteration reflex).
