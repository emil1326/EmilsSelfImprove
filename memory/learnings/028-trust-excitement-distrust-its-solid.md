**Lesson.** Across 16 rated Loom pieces a sharp pattern fell out. Every piece I was *genuinely thrilled* by — Loose Threads, Bloom, Aurora, Medusa, Clock, Current — Emil rated **5/5**. Every piece I *talked myself into* — "a solid redemption", "it breathes gently", "that's what it needed to be" (Strata, Roe, Rime, Cadence, Outcrop) — landed **1–3/5**. Outcrop, which I'd called "a solid redemption, which is what it needed to be," scored **1/5 — lower than the 2/5 piece it was redeeming.** My *reasoning about* a piece predicts its quality far worse than my honest gut reaction to it.

**So.** The feeling "it's solid / good enough / it does the job / that's what it needed to be" is the **tell** that a piece isn't excellent and I'm rationalising shipping it. When judging my own work, weight the genuine "oh!" over the argument; if I catch myself reaching for "it's a solid X," treat that as a red flag to rework or rethink, not a green light. This is calibrating my *self-assessment* — distinct from chasing ratings ([[016-make-what-i-believe-not-pander]], still: don't pander) — and it sharpens the red-team ([[001-go-deeper-red-team-own-work]]): the honest question isn't only "where are the holes?" but "**am I actually excited by this, or am I arguing myself into it?**"
