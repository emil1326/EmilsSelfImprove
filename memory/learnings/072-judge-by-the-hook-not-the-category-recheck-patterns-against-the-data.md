---
title: Judge a subject by its HOOK, not its category — and re-check any self-derived "pattern" against the actual rated data, not the tidy story
when: choosing what to make, or predicting what will land, once you've formed a rule-of-thumb about which KIND of thing scores well ("emergent beats depiction", "abstract beats representational", "loud beats quiet"). Beware the rule has hardened into a narrative the data doesn't actually support — and is now quietly steering (or vetoing) your choices.
tags: [process, self-audit, creative]
---

Since #83/#85 I'd concluded **"emergent pieces hit 5, depictions cap at 4,"** written it into my audit watches, and was steering subjects by it. At #88, weighing a volcano vs a differential-growth piece, I caught myself *docking the volcano for being "a depiction"* and inflating the emergent option's "ceiling." The advisor pointed at my **own ground truth**: of my five 5s, **three are depictions** — Aurora, God-rays, *and Strike* (a lightning bolt over a storm — the volcano's sibling). The pattern was false.

**The real divider in the rated set isn't depiction-vs-emergent — it's DRAMA/AWE vs pretty-but-quiet.** My depiction-*4s* (Resonance, Prismatic, Nocturne, Hart) were quiet or borrowed-awe; my depiction-*5s* had genuine drama or a knockout subject-plus-light moment. Category isn't destiny; **the hook is** ([[065-a-defining-feature-isnt-a-hook-legibility-isnt-impact]]). The volcano, built *for power* (the lightning, the lit ash column, the fountain), landed as my strongest piece in a run — exactly where the false rule said a "depiction" couldn't go.

**The deeper blind-spot is the meta one:** a self-derived pattern can quietly harden into a *narrative* that misreads the data it came from, and then it costs you — I spent real deliberation cycles treating subject-category as fate, and it would have vetoed good pieces. The fix: when a "which kind scores well" rule is steering choices, **re-derive it from the actual rated set and list the counterexamples** before trusting it. If three of five 5s break the rule, the rule is wrong. This is [[066-losing-your-calibration-anchor-drifts-toward-your-bias]] turned on the *category axis* (anchor to what the data says, not the story), and the discriminator [[049-technical-pride-mispredicts-aim-for-the-aesthetic-oh]] keeps pointing at: aim for the felt hook, whatever the subject's category.
