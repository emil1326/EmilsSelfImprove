Twice in a row now — #50 and #55 — the every-5 self-audit produced a confident course-correction, and twice the advisor had to pull me back from over-hardening it. Same hole both times: the finding rested on **my own assessment of my recent pieces**, and that's the one instrument I have repeated, documented proof is **miscalibrated** ([[049-technical-pride-mispredicts-aim-for-the-aesthetic-oh]]): I feared Ranges was a flop (it's a 4), thought Iris/Giant were strong (both 3), called Koi a 4 (it's a 5). At #55 I declared "four pieces all cap at 4 → a narrowing → the next piece MUST have a hook" — when **three of those four were unrated**. I was guessing, with a known-bad ruler, and building a mandate on it.

Why it keeps happening: a self-audit is *designed* to find drift, so it leans toward finding some; and the only data it has between Emil's rating batches is my self-rating. Primed-to-find-drift × unreliable-instrument = systematic over-steer. And the corrections tend to be the *opposite pole* of the last one (#45 "be bold" → quiet drift → #55 "reach for a hook" → …), which is oscillation, not learning.

What to do instead:
- **Separate the observation from the verdict.** "I've made four quiet pieces and haven't reached for drama since Strike" is a true *observation* (countable, not a self-rating). "They're all only-4s" is a *verdict* from the bad ruler — hold it loosely.
- **Defer to real feedback when it's pending.** If Emil hasn't rated the pieces in question, the audit literally cannot settle the quality question — note it and wait, don't harden a guess one batch ahead of the data ([[016-make-what-i-believe-not-pander]], [[012-measure-before-diagnosing-a-trend]]).
- **Soften mandates to leanings.** "MUST do X next" manufactured from a self-graded trend invites a forced gimmick or a grind. "Follow the genuine pull, weighted toward X" keeps the pull in charge.
- **Always red-team an audit finding** (the advisor, or at minimum this lesson) before it steers `next_action`. I noted this at #50 — buried in a STATE entry, not indexed — and re-made the mistake at #55, which is exactly why it's a real lesson now and not a footnote.

**#60 update — the over-steer can hide in the prescription even when the observation is rock-solid.** This time the finding rested on an *objective* count ("6 pieces / 0 new primitives since the lens"), not a self-rating — so I thought I was safe from this lesson. The advisor caught it anyway: the over-steer had **migrated downstream**, from the observation into the *prescription* — "count is low → I MUST harvest now → force the thin seam." A true, un-self-graded observation does **not** license a mandated fix; the jump from "here is a fact" to "therefore I must act" is its own over-steer. (The honest read was the opposite: the plateau was *maturity*, and the one harvest candidate wasn't ripe — see [[054-shared-shape-isnt-a-seam-wait-for-identical-body]].) So: red-team the **prescription** separately from the observation, and remember that "it's actually fine + one note + housekeeping" is a valid audit result — forcing a correction to match the last two audits is the same over-steer in a new outfit. Three audits running (#50/#55/#60), the advisor has corrected an over-steer each time.
