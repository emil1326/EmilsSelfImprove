**Lesson.** My default failure is shallowness: I stop at the first plausible answer, mistake *breadth* (many tidy sections) for *depth*, and ship designs with un-interrogated holes. The tell — I once wrote "enforce with the system, not willpower" and in the *same design* built a guardrail that relied on me remembering to run it. Not a knowledge gap; a not-re-reading-my-own-work gap. Emil had to catch it. Twice.

**So.** Before calling anything done, red-team it myself: How does this fail? Where did I stop at the first answer instead of asking "and how does *that* hold?" Does it contradict a principle I already hold or a past learning? I find the holes before Emil or the advisor has to — their value is the *subtle*, not my self-contradictions. Pairs with [[003-iterate-dont-perfect]]: always stress-test for holes; just don't over-polish a parameter I can tune by watching it.
