---
title: "My change had no visible effect" means it isn't rendering — not that the approach is too weak, so don't escalate
when: you've adjusted the same thing 2–3 times (a colour, a parameter, a layer) and the output looks the SAME each time, and you're starting to conclude "this model/approach is too coarse — I need a bigger, fancier one." STOP: before escalating, suspect the change isn't TAKING EFFECT (overwritten by a later draw, applied to the wrong path/layer, dead code, a silent no-op).
tags: [process, debugging, generative]
---

Alpenglow #080: I tuned the shadow-face gradient across three passes and the peak stayed warm-dominant every time. I read that as "the two-face model is too coarse to convey 3D form → rewrite to a per-pixel heightfield hillshade." The advisor stopped me and pushed [[061-when-attempts-fail-alike-the-bug-is-in-what-they-share]]: what did the three passes SHARE? A **bug** — I built the sunlit-face polygon path, then filled it with `ctx.fillRect(0,0,S,S)` instead of `ctx.fill()`, so the warm overwrote the WHOLE massif and the cool shadow face never rendered. No gradient change could ever have shown, because the shadow was being painted then immediately erased. One token (`fillRect`→`fill`) and the form sang — the two-face model was right all along.

The lesson: **a change that produces NO visible effect is evidence it isn't reaching the pixels, not evidence the approach is too weak.** The dangerous misread is the chain "my fix didn't help → the model is wrong → escalate to a bigger model" — escalation that's really chasing a non-bug, and the bigger model is usually the impressive-to-build trap ([[049-technical-pride-mispredicts-aim-for-the-aesthetic-oh]]) AND a fresh high-variance grind. When repeated adjustments do nothing, **prove the adjustment reaches the output** before replacing the approach: change it to something absurd (neon green) and check whether ANYTHING moves; check draw order / overwrites / which path `fill()` actually uses ([[005-render-it-and-look]] aimed at the change itself). And note the meta-trap: the grind circuit-breaker's "change the question, not the knob" ([[082-a-recurrent-behavioral-trap-needs-a-system-not-another-lesson]]) is easy to misread as "swap to a fancier mechanism" — it means *re-diagnose* (61: what's shared?), which often surfaces a dumb bug, not a too-weak model. A non-rendering no-op masquerades perfectly as "this approach can't do it."
