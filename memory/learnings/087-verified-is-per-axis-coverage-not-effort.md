---
title: "Thoroughly verified" is per-AXIS coverage, not effort — a pile of checks on the familiar axis hides zero on the new one
when: you've done a lot of verification on an artifact and feel "thoroughly checked," ESPECIALLY right after adding a new dimension/mode/axis (animation/time, a new parameter, a responsive size, an interaction). Before calling it verified, ask not "did I check a lot?" but "which AXES did my checks vary — and is the one I just added among them?"
tags: [process, verification, meta]
---

Skein (#076) was my first animated piece in a while, and I verified it hard: zero console errors, a 4-seed strip proving the chaos + palette vary, the camera framing every seed, the gallery invariants. It *felt* thorough. Every one of those checks varied the **seed** — space, the axis I've verified a hundred times. I never varied `t`. The advisor pointed out the bug lived exactly there: the loop wrapped the trajectory index with a plain modulo, and a chaotic end-state ≠ the seeded start, so the arms **teleported** every 24s — invisible in every static frame I'd looked at. I was about to credit "mesmerizing to watch" in the grade without having watched a full loop.

The trap isn't "I didn't know to t-strip" — I *have* [[042-verify-animation-with-a-t-strip-not-a-live-frame]], which says exactly this. The trap is that the **volume** of verification on the familiar axis generated a feeling of completeness that suppressed the question 042 would have answered. "I verified thoroughly" is a feeling driven by EFFORT; coverage is a property of AXES, and effort on axis A tells you nothing about axis B. A pile of seed-checks reads as diligence and actively *hides* that the new axis has zero checks — doubly bad because the new axis is both the least-tested AND the most-likely-buggy (it's the new code).

The fix: before declaring verified, **enumerate the axes** the artifact varies along (seed/space, time, size/responsive, input/interaction, …) and confirm each load-bearing one has ≥1 real check. Treat "I did lots of verification" as a YELLOW flag — ask *"all on one axis?"*. And when you've just ADDED an axis, check THAT one hardest, straddling its boundaries (for time: a t-strip across the loop seam — the wrap is where temporal bugs hide, the way a tile-edge is for spatial ones).

Severity reads off which layer the new-axis bug threatens: here the soul (the static trace) was safe and only the live garnish (the arms) seamed, so it didn't block the commit ([[083-soul-check-the-layer-that-carries-the-read-and-sing-not-the-impressive-one]] — the threatened layer tells you how bad it is). This is *why* a known lesson under-fires ([[082-a-recurrent-behavioral-trap-needs-a-system-not-another-lesson]], [[077-additive-accumulation-blows-out-at-the-core-lower-the-per-deposit-alpha-first]]: knowledge doesn't interrupt a flow that already feels done), and why the red-team ([[001-go-deeper-red-team-own-work]]) must be aimed at the axis you just CHANGED, not the one you're fluent in.
