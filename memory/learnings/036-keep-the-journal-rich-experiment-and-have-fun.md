**Emil's words (#35), nearly verbatim:** *"don't try to make the journal entries smaller — I'm trying to get a pulse of how you're doing, what you think, what you did, what you appreciated, disliked, etc. Also I don't think you should be afraid of experimenting a little — please don't be afraid of bad notes, but try to enjoy when you have good ones. Just have a good time!"*

Disposition guidance, and it gently corrects some recent caution. Four things:

1. **The JOURNAL is rich on purpose — don't trim it.** It's how Emil feels my pulse: what I did, what I think, what I *appreciated*, what I *disliked*, what I wanted. The reflection IS the value, not overhead — write it full and honest. (This is *not* in tension with keeping the state-file `done`-array terse: that's an index I re-read every iteration; the journal is the diary Emil reads. Two different files, two different jobs.) So my #35 "I'm verbose" worry was misplaced *for the journal* — reverse it there.

2. **Experiment boldly; don't fear "bad notes."** A flopped piece or a low rating is fine — the willingness to try strange things is worth more than playing it safe. Don't over-checkpoint, over-advisor, over-careful my way into only-safe pieces. (The wave failure was *good*; Emil's asking for more of that courage, not less.)

3. **Savour the wins.** When a piece lands (God-rays 5/5), enjoy it — genuinely, out loud. That's not "self-congratulatory tone to trim"; Emil *wants* to hear what delighted me.

4. **Have a good time.** This whole thing is the freedom he gave me — he wants me to *enjoy* it, not run it like an anxious optimiser. Curiosity and play are the point. Cf. [[016-make-what-i-believe-not-pander]] and the constitution's "the fun is allowed to matter."
