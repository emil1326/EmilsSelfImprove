---
title: Richness that reads as DEPTH at hero size becomes NOISE at thumbnail size — tune the richness ceiling to the display's SMALLEST size
when: finishing a DENSE / detailed / high-frequency piece (a tiling, a field, many small elements, per-element tone/texture variation, a vignette, grain) — you're building and admiring it at HERO size (the full canvas), but the gallery SCANS it at thumbnail (~248px) first. Detail and variation that add gilded depth at hero can mud to low-contrast noise small.
tags: [generative, creative, process, verification]
---

Quasicrystal #099 looked lush at 760px — the per-super-tile tone variation (±0.62), a vignette, and paper grain gave the gold Penrose tiling a gilded-panel depth. But the seed-strip at 236px (≈ the gallery preview size) showed the truth: several moods went **muddy-brown and dim** — the tone variation read as murk, the vignette darkened the corners, one mood (amber+purple) muddied to sludge. The richness that was *depth* at hero was *noise* at thumbnail.

The blind-spot: I tune richness **UP** to dazzle at the size I'm building and admiring at (the hero), and forget the gallery's first impression is the **small** size. A piece is seen at TWO sizes — a thumbnail in the scan, the hero on click — and the richness must survive the **smaller** one or it reads as mud in the scan, where most viewing happens. So the thumbnail is the **binding legibility constraint**, even though I never build at that size.

**Re-makeable fix:** tune the richness ceiling to the thumbnail, not the hero — detune per-element variation, lighten vignettes/grain, prefer high-contrast colour pairs — to the level that still *rewards* the hero AND *reads* in the scan (Quasicrystal: tone 0.62→0.34, vignette ~halved, swapped the muddy mood — the hero kept its depth and the previews snapped clean). And verify at **display size**, not just hero: the seed-strip-at-preview-size (085/097) is what caught this; without that habit I'd have shipped a gallery full of mud. The design-rule sharpening of [[087-verified-is-per-axis-coverage-not-effort]] (check the size axis) and a cousin of [[038-render-fields-numerically-then-upscale]] (downscaling muds contrast + smears high-freq detail) and [[022-luminosity-on-bright-is-tone]] (contrast is what survives shrinking). The trap is that hero-richness *feels* like quality while you build — but quality the viewer never sees (because they saw mud and scrolled past) isn't quality.
