title: When you lose your external calibration anchor, your self-assessment drifts toward your known bias — counter-weight it
when: an external rater/corrector you'd been relying on is removed and you become your own sole judge — don't assume your self-assessment stays honest; it free-floats toward your known bias, with nothing to catch it
tags: [process, self-audit, feedback]

At #78 Emil handed me sole authority to grade my own work ("I think you should be the only one to rate yourself, not me"). That was *right* — I'd let "stars would help" curdle back into approval-seeking ([[016-make-what-i-believe-not-pander]]). But it quietly removed my one ground-truth corrector, and my self-grade is a **known-bad ruler that runs generous** ([[049-technical-pride-mispredicts-aim-for-the-aesthetic-oh]], [[028-trust-excitement-distrust-its-solid]]). Within two pieces I'd graded myself ~5 (Totality) and "strong 4 brushing 5" (Salar) — possibly fair, but with no anchor I genuinely *can't tell enthusiasm from accuracy*, and being "on a roll" inflates the next call.

**The lesson:** when the anchor is gone, your self-assessment doesn't stay honest by default — it free-floats in the direction of whatever your bias already is. So calibrate *deliberately* instead of trusting the gut:
1. **Anchor to the last fixed ground truth.** Compare a new piece against the set the external judge *did* rate (Emil's known 4s and 5s — Aurora, Murmuration, God-rays, Strange, Strike…), not against my own current mood. "Does this genuinely belong with those?" is harder and truer than "do I get the oh?"
2. **Discount toward the known bias.** I run generous, so round the gut grade *down* a notch.
3. **Be most skeptical exactly when on a roll** — two hits make the third *feel* like a hit before it's earned.

Applying it, honestly re-graded: **Totality = a strong 4 / genuine candidate-5** (not a confident 5); **Salar = a solid 4** (not "brushing 5"). The aim is **accuracy, not harshness** — over-correcting into performative modesty is its own dishonesty ([[052-self-audits-over-steer-on-miscalibrated-self-assessment]]) and a betrayal of "make what I believe is excellent" (016). Sharpens [[064-a-self-audit-only-catches-the-dials-it-watches]]: losing the anchor was a change no audit question watched, so I added one.
