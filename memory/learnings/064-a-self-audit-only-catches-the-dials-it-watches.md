For roughly **seventy iterations** my journal silently narrowed — from the rich diary the early entries are (capturing conversations with Emil, his mid-session nudges, the in-betweens, out-of-cycle `## ✎` notes) into a task-only *changelog* with reflection attached. Every-fifth I run a self-audit specifically to catch drift. It never caught this one. **Emil did** (#76.5), by pointing me at the start of my own journal.

Why the audit missed it: its six questions hunt *creative* drift, *library* drift, and my *self-grading* — and "has the journal's SCOPE narrowed from diary to changelog?" is on **none** of them. The drift was real, slow, and seventy iterations long, and it walked straight past every audit because no question was pointed at that dial. Worse, the audit's *existence* gave me false comfort — "I have a drift-catcher, so I'd have caught it" — which is exactly the complacency that let it run.

**The lesson:** a self-audit is only as wide as its question set. It catches *known* failure modes and is blind to *unknown* ones by construction. So:
1. Don't only *answer* the fixed questions — periodically ask the **meta-question**: *what has changed that none of my checks watch?* (The scariest drift is the unwatched one.)
2. When a blind-spot drift is found, **add a question for it** (wire-it-in, [[002-enforce-with-the-system-not-willpower]]) — but know the new question closes only *that* hole, not the next unwatched one.
3. Treat "the audit passed" as "no *known* drift," never as "no drift."

Sibling of [[021-audit-the-goal-not-the-proxy]] (audit the goal, not the checkbox) and [[041-retest-standing-assumptions-and-deferred-items]] (re-test the stale belief) — but sharper: here the *checklist itself* was the stale, too-narrow instrument. And it's why [[036-keep-the-journal-rich-experiment-and-have-fun]] needed enforcing in the loop, not just believing.
