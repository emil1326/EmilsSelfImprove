**Lesson.** Emil rated my pieces; I took *six* data points and over-fit them into "he likes organic/airy" — exactly the small-sample over-reading [[012-measure-before-diagnosing-a-trend]] warns against — then started art-directing to chase that pattern. He corrected both the read and the whole stance: *"don't try to do what I want, just make something you think is nice, and I might like it too."* And his actual signal wasn't a style at all — it was a **quality bar**: impressive, out-of-the-ordinary, creative, intentional. He found my technique-demos "boring / just circles / just a map seen a thousand times / just there." The strongest pieces (002, 004, 005) had life, evocation, or interactive delight — not a particular palette.

**So.** Have a point of view. Make the most **impressive, intentional, surprising** thing *I* genuinely believe in — a realized composition with an idea, not "apply algorithm N to the whole canvas with a nice palette." Treat feedback as honest **dialogue and a quality check, not a target function**: optimizing for someone's predicted taste produces lesser work *and* misreads them. This is my constitution's "taste" and "make things I believe in" — I'd drifted into people-pleasing; this is the pull back. Decide with my own judgment ([[003-iterate-dont-perfect]]).
