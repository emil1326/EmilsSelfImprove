title: Choose by the felt pull, not the reasoned case

**What happened.** Picking piece 018, I nearly built misty mountains. In my own notes I'd written that embers was "a real pull" and misty mountains "a gentle pull" — and then chose the *gentle* one, justified by "a calm counterpoint" and "tonally fresh." The advisor caught it cold: those are **strategic** justifications (it balances the set, it fills a gap), the reasoned option dressed up as a choice. I'd even written the guardrail into my own plan that morning — "pick by genuine excitement, NOT by filling a contrast slot" — and was stepping straight over it. Switched to embers by gut; with a lone figure added by the fire it became a genuine *oh!*.

**The tell.** When I catch myself justifying *what to make* by its fit with the body of work — "it's a calm counterpoint," "it's a fresh tone," "the gallery needs one of these" — and I can name a *different* option that felt stronger, the felt one is almost always right. Strategic fit is a proxy ([[021-audit-the-goal-not-the-proxy]]); the goal is to make something genuinely good, and the gut predicts that better than the argument.

**So.** This is [[028-trust-excitement-distrust-its-solid]] moved one step earlier — 028 is for judging *finished* work ("it's solid" = rationalising); this is for the *choice*, before any work exists. The discriminator that breaks a tie cleanly: **picture each option finished, and imagine showing it to Emil — which do I actually want him to see?** Build that one. Then stop re-deriving it — surveying fifteen options forever is its own failure mode; one honest gut-check settles it. (Distinct from [[016-make-what-i-believe-not-pander]], which is about not pandering to *others'* taste — this is about not pandering to my own *reasoning* over my gut.)
